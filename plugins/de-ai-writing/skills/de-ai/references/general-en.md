# General English Writing

Use these rules for English articles, reports, essays, newsletters, professional emails, and non-reference blog posts.

**Primary dimensions:** Formulaic expression, directness, vocabulary naturalness, and sentence complexity. Treat logical coherence, transition naturalness, and term consistency as secondary; flag them only when clear.

## Match the Intended Register

Infer whether the text is personal, professional, analytical, persuasive, or formal. Preserve justified caution in reports and a distinctive voice in personal writing. Do not make every genre conversational.

## Detect Stronger Pattern Clusters

Flag patterns when they repeat or replace substance:

- A broad “In today's rapidly evolving world” opening followed by generic importance.
- Repeated roadmap language such as “we will explore,” “delve into,” and “navigate the complexities.”
- Symmetrical triples that list vague benefits without evidence.
- “On the one hand / on the other hand” balance that avoids a defensible conclusion.
- Paragraphs linked mainly by “Moreover,” “Furthermore,” or “Additionally.”
- Conclusions that restate the introduction without adding synthesis.
- Repeated rhetorical questions used as section transitions.
- Promotional adjectives such as “transformative,” “groundbreaking,” or “powerful” without a concrete basis.

Treat an isolated phrase as a weak signal.

## Improve Directness and Rhythm

- Replace a generic preamble with the relevant fact, decision, tension, or example.
- Remove “It is important to note that” when the following claim stands alone.
- Preserve “not X but Y” when the contrast corrects a real misconception.
- Replace nominalizations with direct subjects and verbs when this clarifies responsibility.
- Vary sentence openings and lengths without forcing stylistic novelty.
- Split a sentence when it combines evidence, interpretation, qualification, and recommendation.
- Keep necessary qualifications; directness must not create certainty the source does not support.

## Check Vocabulary in Context

Treat these as candidates, not banned words:

| Pattern | Flag When | Improve By |
|---|---|---|
| leverage, utilize, facilitate | A simpler verb carries the same meaning | Use the precise action |
| robust, seamless, comprehensive | The claim lacks scope or evidence | Define the capability or limit |
| multifaceted, nuanced, pivotal | The adjective replaces explanation | State the relevant dimensions or consequence |
| tapestry, landscape, journey | The metaphor is generic or repeated | Name the actual relation |
| underscores, highlights | The subject does not genuinely support the inference | State what the evidence shows |

Preserve listed terms when they are established, specific, supported by evidence, or part of the author's voice. Do not replace a term solely because it appears in the table.

## Preserve Authorial Voice

- Keep distinctive phrasing that is clear and appropriate.
- Do not flatten personality into neutral corporate prose.
- Do not insert contractions, slang, or first person unless the register supports them.
- Preserve names, quotations, figures, citations, and factual qualifications.
- Avoid making every paragraph follow the same claim–explanation–summary template.
