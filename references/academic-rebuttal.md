# Academic Rebuttals and Reviewer Responses

Use these rules for rebuttals, response letters, author responses, and point-by-point replies to reviewers.

**Primary dimensions:** Formulaic expression and directness. Give secondary attention to logical coherence and term consistency because they protect answer structure, claims, and commitments; flag other dimensions only when clear.

## Organize Each Response

Prefer this order when the content supports it:

1. Answer the concern directly.
2. Give the evidence, reasoning, or clarification.
3. State the manuscript change, with a location when available.
4. Acknowledge any remaining limitation.

Do not force all four parts when a short answer is sufficient.

## Detect Rebuttal-Specific AI Flavor

Flag patterns when they repeat or obscure the answer:

- Repeated generic gratitude across multiple responses: “We sincerely thank the reviewer for this insightful and valuable comment.”
- Long acknowledgments that paraphrase the concern before answering it.
- Mechanical headings and identical response openings across all reviewers.
- Excessive apology or deference that makes the authors sound uncertain about supported claims.
- Defensive language such as “the reviewer misunderstood,” “obviously,” or “as clearly stated.”
- Vague assurances such as “we have significantly improved the manuscript” without naming the change.
- Future-tense promises presented as completed work.
- Repeated “we would like to emphasize/clarify/note” where the claim can be stated directly.

Treat brief thanks, explicit clarification, and repeated point-by-point structure as normal rebuttal conventions.
Treat defensiveness as a high-priority revision target, not merely a phrase-level style preference. Remove it whenever the factual answer can stand alone. Do not weaken a supported disagreement into agreement; state the disagreement, evidence, and scope without blame, self-justification, or claims about the reviewer's understanding.

## Keep Politeness Specific

- Use one brief acknowledgment when it serves the interaction.
- Refer to the substance of the concern rather than praising every comment.
- Replace blame with neutral clarification. When the factual answer can stand alone, prefer “To clarify, all methods use X” over any form of “the setup was misunderstood.”
- Preserve disagreement when justified, but support it with evidence or scope.
- Avoid both combative certainty and performative humility.

## Protect Claims and Commitments

- Distinguish completed changes (“We added…”) from intended changes (“We will add…”).
- Treat unsupported self-evaluation such as “significantly improved the manuscript” as rhetoric, not as a factual commitment. Preserve the verifiable action (“we revised the manuscript”) and remove the ungrounded degree word.
- When the source says only that a revision was made, keep that statement neutral and flag the missing location or detail for confirmation; do not invent specificity or add intensifiers such as “substantially.”
- Preserve experiment settings, numbers, statistical qualifiers, citations, and section references.
- Do not claim that a new experiment resolves a concern unless its result actually addresses that concern.
- Do not strengthen “suggests” into “demonstrates” or a limited result into a general claim.
- Keep limitations visible when they matter to the reviewer's question.
- Do not invent page numbers, line numbers, experiments, or manuscript edits.

## Improve Common Response Patterns

| Pattern | Prefer |
|---|---|
| “We thank the reviewer for pointing out this important issue. We would like to clarify that…” | “The reported result uses X, not Y.” |
| “We respectfully disagree with the reviewer.” | State the disagreement and evidence directly, using “respectfully” only if tone requires it |
| “We have significantly improved the manuscript accordingly.” | “We revised the manuscript accordingly.” |
| “We have revised the manuscript accordingly.” | Name the revision and its location |
| “Due to space limitations…” | State the actual scope or constraint and what is now included |
| “This is beyond the scope of this paper.” | Explain the boundary and why it does not undermine the evaluated claim |

## Preserve Rebuttal Function

- Keep reviewer identifiers, quoted concerns, response numbering, and venue formatting intact.
- Keep the answer easy to locate; do not hide it beneath background.
- Retain necessary politeness and hedging.
- Match the venue's length limit and response conventions when known.
- Do not turn a substantive rebuttal into generic diplomatic prose.
